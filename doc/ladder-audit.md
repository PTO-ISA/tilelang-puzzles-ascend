# Ladder audit — does each variant make a meaningful, incremental change?

Each variant should add one production config and teach one idea. This page
measures whether each one does, and records which ones do not. It is a **report**: the only
structural change made from it so far is the `cast_back` output-dtype merge
described below.

## How this is measured, and what the numbers cannot tell you

- **Solution lines** — substantive (non-blank, non-comment) lines inside the
  `BEGIN/END SOLUTION` region, which is exactly what the student writes.
- **Delta** — changed lines against the *preceding* variant in the same kernel
  and tier (insertions plus deletions over an aligned diff of the two regions).
- **New intrinsics** — operations used in this variant's region that no earlier
  variant in the same kernel used. Zero means it teaches no new instruction.
  The `new ASC` column counts `S.*`; the `new SIMT` column counts `T.*`
  builtins, because SIMT has no vector intrinsics and does its arithmetic with
  plain operators. **The two columns are not comparable to each other** — each
  answers "does this variant need a primitive its kernel has not needed before"
  within its own tier.

The table is generated: `python tools/ladder_table.py --write`, checked by
`python tools/ladder_table.py --check`. It was hand-maintained until the SIMT
tier needed a fourth column, and generating it is what stops the published
numbers drifting from the files.

Two caveats that matter more than the numbers:

1. **The ladder is not monotone.** Several variants revert to an earlier body,
   so "delta against the predecessor" overstates them. `per_block/04`'s delta
   against its predecessor is 22 lines, but against `01` it is **zero** — the
   regions are byte-identical. Where that happens it is called out.
2. **Region size is not information content.** `per_token/03`'s ASC region is one
   line, but that line is the packed UE8M0 store and the rest of the change lives
   outside the region in buffer dtypes. It teaches a real thing through a weak
   exercise. The fix for that class is to *widen the region*, not to merge.
3. **Line counts are not comparable across tiers**, only within one. A torch
   region and a SIMT region of the same length are not equally hard, and the SIMT
   column is additionally *understated* at `per_token/04` and `per_block/03`,
   where an 18-line hand-written e2m1 encoder lives outside the sentinels as a
   module-level helper — exactly as PTO's round-to-odd macro does, but five times
   the size. See [`tiers/simt.md`](tiers/simt.md).

## When to merge two variants

**Merge only when the change is trivial in all four tiers.** Triviality in torch
proves nothing, because a torch parameter routinely becomes a schedule change and
then an instruction change on the way down. The SIMT tier makes the rule bite
harder rather than softer: it is trivial on *more* variants than ASC or PTO are,
so a config that looks mergeable from torch and SIMT together can still be a real
instruction change in the two SIMD tiers. `cast_back/04` is the clearest case —
6 SIMT lines, identical to `cast_back/01`'s — and it stays, because ASC and PTO
genuinely lose `S.pset`, a `BRC_B32` load and an `S.vsel` there.

### Merged: the output dtype (was `cast_back/02_fp32_out`)

| | torch | ASC | PTO | SIMT |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the whole change | drop `.to(bfloat16)` | **one store line** | **one store line** | **nothing at all** |

SIMT does not even have a store line to change: `T.copy` converts between buffers
of different dtypes, so the output dtype is carried entirely by the declared type
of `out_ub` and the VF body never branches on it.

Trivial in all four, so it is now a trace-time `out_dtype` switch on
[cast_back/01](quant/cast_back/01_e4m3_fp32sf.md). The two store paths sit side by
side, which reads better than a two-file diff: `NORM_B32` against `PK_B32`, where
the *wider* dtype takes the *simpler* instruction. `cast_back` renumbered to six
variants.

### Not merged: the `sf_block` family (`cast_back/03`, `/04`)

These look trivial from torch and are not:

| step | torch | ASC | PTO | SIMT |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `(1,32)` → `(32,32)` | a different `.view()` | region identical, but `Sf` changes shape and the loop nest becomes `for m_block / for row` with the scale DMA **hoisted** | same schedule change | same schedule change |
| `(32,32)` → `(32,1)` | a different broadcast | **loses `S.pset`, one `BRC_B32` load and `S.vsel`** — 8 ops to 6 | loses the grouped slot-load + `vbrc` pair | `sf_ub[i // bk]` becomes `sf_ub[i]` |

So the first step is a *schedule* change and the second an *instruction* change.
They stay, and the asymmetry is itself the lesson.

The SIMT column is what makes the second step worth keeping rather than what
makes it mergeable: SIMT's change is one subscript, and that is precisely the
contrast the variant exists to draw. Deleting the variant would delete the
observation that the same configuration costs the SIMD tiers three instructions
and costs the thread model nothing.

### Meets the rule but not yet changed: `per_block/04_col_major_tma`

The strongest remaining candidate, and stronger than the one already merged:

- the ASC and PTO regions are **byte-identical to `per_block/01`** — 0 lines, 0
  tokens. The student retypes 22 (ASC) / 26 (PTO) lines verbatim;
- the hint was a **verbatim copy** of `01`'s, so nothing told them so (now fixed —
  see below);
- torch is 2 real lines (`return q, sf.T.contiguous()`);
- the entire change is outside the region: `SfCm` is `(num_k_blocks,
  num_m_blocks)` and the final `T.copy(sf_ub[0:1], SfCm[kb, mb:mb + 1])` swaps a
  pair of indices.

Trivial in all four tiers — SIMT's region is also byte-identical to `per_block/01`'s
19 lines — so by the rule it should fold into `per_block/01` as a `col_major`
switch. Acting on it means renumbering `per_block`, so it is listed rather than
done.

## Variants that teach a real idea through a weak exercise

Here the region boundary is drawn so the student does not write the new thing.
**The fix is to widen the region, not to merge** — and in the PTO cases it also
restores 1:1:1 alignment with ASC, where the student *does* write the arithmetic.

| variant | the problem |
|---|---|
| `per_token/03` (ASC) | the region is **1 line of 80** in the file: the `PK4_B32` packed store. Everything else is given. |
| `per_token/02` (PTO) | the region is 3 lines calling a `compute_scale` macro **defined above it**. The ASC student writes the ceil-log2 trick by hand; the PTO student does not. |
| `per_token/04` (PTO) | same shape: `to_bf16_round_odd` is provided above the region. |
| `per_token/06` (PTO) | novelty routed through provided macros; zero new intrinsics in the region. |
| `per_channel/02` | 8 (ASC) / 9 (PTO) / 4 (SIMT) lines, against 13 in torch — the NPU exercise is thinner than the torch one, in every vector tier. |

### A short SIMT region is not automatically a weak one

The table bolds any region of four lines or fewer, and two SIMT regions trip that
threshold for opposite reasons. The distinction matters, so it is spelled out
rather than left to the number.

`per_token/02` (SIMT) is **4 lines and not weak**: those four lines *are* the
ceil-log2 exponent trick, which is the whole of what the variant teaches. The
region is narrow because scalar integer arithmetic is terse, not because the
novelty was moved outside the sentinels. It aligns 1:1 with ASC's region, where
the student writes the same arithmetic as vector operations.

`per_channel/02` (SIMT) is **4 lines and genuinely thin**, in the same way and for
the same reason as its ASC and PTO siblings: the region is the pack alone, and the
reduction and scale math above it are given. It is listed in the table above with
them.

### A second deliberate departure: `simt/per_token/04`'s extra keyword

Every tier's `launch(x)` and `compile_kernel(hidden)` are otherwise identical
across torch, ASC, PTO and SIMT — that is what makes the files diffable and the
harness tier-agnostic. `simt/per_token/04` is the one exception: it takes an
additive `branch_free: bool = False` on both, because it ships **two spellings
of the e2m1 encoder** that differ by 1.78x in simulated cycles.

The divergence is deliberately the smallest kind. The keyword is additive with a
default, so `launch(x)` — the call the harness makes on every tier — is
unchanged, and the default path is the one the published measurement reports.
The harness exercises the second arm too and asserts it is byte-identical.

The alternative was to keep the faster spelling only in
`harness/probe/simt_divergence.py`, which would have made it an aside rather
than a reference answer. Shipping both is the better trade: the 1.78x is one of
the more useful things this tier teaches, and a flag in the file is where a
reader will find it. See
[`quant/per_token/04_fp32_in_fp4_out.md`](quant/per_token/04_fp32_in_fp4_out.md).

### One deliberate 1:1 departure: `per_token/03`

Every other variant's SIMT region covers the same stages as its ASC and PTO
siblings'. `per_token/03` is the exception: ASC's region is the famous
**1 line of 80**, and the SIMT region is the whole VF body instead.

That is not an oversight. This page already identifies ASC's one-line region as
the clearest weak exercise in the ladder and says the fix is to *widen the
region*. Copying it into a new tier would have propagated a defect the audit was
written to flag, so the SIMT file is what the recommended fix looks like, and the
mismatch stands as a reminder that ASC and PTO still want the same treatment.

## Steps that are too large

Four consecutive-pair deltas exceed 55 changed lines in both vector tiers:

| step | Δ ASC | Δ PTO | Δ SIMT | what arrives at once |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `per_token/04` → `/05` | 56 | 63 | 22 | `S.vci`, `S.vgather2`, the index-vector transpose |
| `per_token/05` → `/06` | **93** | **72** | 27 | four compile-time modes, a scratch buffer, two barriers |
| `per_token/06` → `/07` | 68 | 62 | 27 | `S.vld2`, `S.vcgmax`, bfloat16 compute at 128 lanes |
| `per_channel/03` → `/04` | 59 | 68 | 19 | 7 new ASC intrinsics in one variant |

`per_token/01` → `/02` is also 46 lines in ASC: the exponent trick arrives as five
new intrinsics together. Splitting any of these means inventing a new intermediate
variant — new material rather than restructuring — so none is recommended without
a decision on what the intermediate step should teach.

**No SIMT step is too large.** The largest Δ SIMT in the ladder is 27 lines, against
93 in ASC, and the steps the SIMD tiers find hardest are among the gentlest here —
`/04` → `/05` is the in-register transpose, which the thread model does not have.
That is a real property of the tier and not a tuning artefact, but it cuts both
ways: a gentle ladder is a ladder that teaches less per step, and three of the
SIMT steps that look comfortable (`per_token/05`, `/07`, `per_channel/03`)
introduce **no new primitive at all** in the `new SIMT` column. A student working
only this tier would never meet a gather, a lane mask or a distribution mode,
because the thread model has no use for them.

## Fixed in this pass: seven misleading hints

A hint that describes code other than the answer is worse than no hint. Found by
comparing every hint's named operations against its own answer body:

| variant | was |
|---|---|
| `per_block/02` (ASC, PTO) | a **verbatim copy of `01`'s hint**, telling the student to "divide both ways" where the answer uses the exponent trick (`vmuls`/`vshrs`/`vadds`/`vshls`, and no `vdiv` at all) |
| `per_block/03` (ASC, PTO) | the same copied hint, with no mention of the FP4 path the answer actually takes (`vdintlv`/`vor`/`vmin`) |
| `per_block/04` (ASC, PTO) | the same copied hint again — accurate by luck, since the body *is* `01`'s, but it never said so |
| `per_token/04` (ASC) | told the student to use `S.vmins`, which **does not compile** on this toolchain (`asc_min_scalar` has no uint16 overload) |
| `per_token/02`, `/04` (PTO) | told the student to "write a macro" that is already provided above the region |

All now describe their own answer. The check is mechanical and worth re-running
after any edit to a hint or a solution body.
